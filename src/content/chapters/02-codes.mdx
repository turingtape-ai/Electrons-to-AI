Chappe sent numbers that pointed into a book. Samuel Morse, a few decades later, sent short and long pulses that spelled words letter by letter. This chapter follows the idea of a code from one to the other and asks why a code built from only two states, stretched out in time, turned out to be the one that lasted.

It will also look at what makes a code *good*: short signs for the commonest letters, a way to tell where one sign ends and the next begins, and a way to notice when something has gone wrong in transit.

The interactive will be a key you tap to send letters, and a decoder that reads them back.
