In 1965 an engineer noticed that the number of transistors that could be put on a chip was doubling every year or two, and guessed it would continue. It did, for a very long time. This chapter looks at what that doubling bought, what it cost, what finally slowed it, and why so much of the software and learning in later Parts depends on it.

The interactive will be a slider across the decades, showing how many switches fit in a fingernail.
