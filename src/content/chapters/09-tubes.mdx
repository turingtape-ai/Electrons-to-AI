Relays are slow because iron is heavy. This chapter introduces the vacuum tube, which does the relay's job with a stream of electrons and a small voltage on a wire grid, thousands of times faster. It also looks at what a room full of tubes cost in heat, electricity and failures, and why that cost drove the search for something better.

The interactive will show a tube's grid voltage opening and closing the flow of current.
